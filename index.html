About

Product Designer · Hanoi

I started by drawing screens. Now I design the rules behind them.

My first work was mobile UI/UX: a driver app, and two connected apps for MB Bank. Over time it moved upstream, from how a screen looks to how a feature should behave.

Today I design a retail and F&B management platform, where one feature often touches the POS, the back office and the mobile app at once. That taught me to ask what problem we are solving before deciding what the screen looks like.

Outside work I'm usually trying a new AI tool or workflow, which is how I ended up with AI certificates from Anthropic and Google. The rest of the time I'm at the gym or playing sports.

Approach(06)

First I get the requirement right with Product and BA. The screens come after the logic is agreed.

Step 1

Clarify the brief

Read the ticket and business rules, then settle open questions with the PO and BA before drawing.

with PO · BA
Step 2

Map flows & states

Happy path first, then the edge cases and errors a real shop will run into.

Step 3

Wireframe the logic

Low-fidelity screens to agree how the feature behaves before any visual polish.

Step 4

Prototype fast

Clickable prototypes built with AI help, so a flow can be checked early.

AI-assisted
Step 5

Hand off & design QA

Walk Dev and QA through the logic, review test cases, check the build and log UI issues.

with Dev · QA
Step 6

Follow up after release

Track feedback and bug reports after launch, then fix what still gets in the way in the next iteration.

  • Success
  • Loading
  • Empty
  • Partial data
  • Very long content
  • No permission
  • Offline
  • Validation error
  • System error
